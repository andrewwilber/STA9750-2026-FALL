#' Acquire Netflix What We Watched Reports
#' 
#' This function will acquire and standardize all data for MP#01
#' from Netflix. Because Netflix hosts the data files behind a CDN, we
#' are accessing the release pages and automatically extracting links to the
#' relevant files (with CDN hash). 
#' 
#' After import, files are stacked vertically and some basic cleaning is
#' performed. 
load_netflix_data <- function(){
  # Note to students: Make sure you have installed the tidyverse and fs packages
  #                   before beginning this assignment. If you aren't sure,
  #                   simply run `library(tidyverse)` at your R Console and, if
  #                   you don't see an error message, `tidyverse` has been 
  #                   installed. (Same for `fs`) If you still need to install
  #                   either package, use the `install.packages` function as 
  #                   demonstrated in class.
  library(tidyverse)
  library(rvest)
  library(readxl)
  library(fs)
  library(skimr)
  library(scales)
  library(gt)
  library(ggtext)
  
  data_dir <- path("data", "mp01")
    
  if(!dir_exists(data_dir)){
    dir_create(data_dir, showWarnings=FALSE, recurse=TRUE)
  }
  
  NETFLIX_URLS <- list(
    `2023H1` = "https://about.netflix.com/news/what-we-watched-a-netflix-engagement-report",
    `2023H2` = "https://about.netflix.com/news/what-we-watched-the-second-half-of-2023",
    `2024H1` = "https://about.netflix.com/news/what-we-watched-the-first-half-of-2024",
    `2024H2` = "https://about.netflix.com/news/what-we-watched-the-second-half-of-2024",
    `2025H1` = "https://about.netflix.com/news/what-we-watched-the-first-half-of-2025",
    `2025H2` = "https://about.netflix.com/news/what-we-watched-the-second-half-of-2025",
    `2026H1` = "https://about.netflix.com/news/what-we-watched-the-first-half-of-2026"
  )
  
  download_if_needed <- function(url, local){
    if(!file_exists(local)){
      download.file(url, local, quiet = TRUE, mode = "wb")
    }
    
    return(local)
  }
    
  NETFLIX_URLS |> 
    map(read_html) |> 
    map(html_element, xpath = "//a[contains(@href, '.xlsx')]") |> 
    map_chr(html_attr, "href") |> 
    enframe("period", "url") |> 
   mutate(path = path(data_dir, paste0(period, ".xlsx")), 
          path = map2(url, path, download_if_needed), 
          data = map(path, read_excel, skip=3, na="*"), 
          data = map(data, \(x) x |> mutate(`Release Date` = as.Date(`Release Date`)))) |>
    unnest(data) |> 
    select(-url, -path) |> 
    mutate(year=str_sub(period, 1, 4), 
           half=str_sub(period, start=6), 
           period_start = make_date(year, if_else(half==1, 1, 7), 1), 
           period_end = make_date(year, if_else(half==1, 7, 12), if_else(half==1, 30, 31))) |> 
    filter(!is.na(`Hours Viewed`)) |> 
    mutate(live_event = !is.na(Runtime) & str_detect(Runtime, "\\d+:\\d+\\*"), 
           clean_time = str_remove(Runtime, fixed("*")),
           Runtime = 60 * hour(hm(clean_time)) + minute(hm(clean_time))) |>
    select(-clean_time) |>
    mutate(Runtime = coalesce(Runtime, mean(Runtime, na.rm=TRUE)), 
           .by=Title, 
           Title = stringi::stri_trans_general(Title, "Latin-ASCII"),
           Title = str_remove(Title, " // .*"), 
           Title = str_remove(Title, "[^[:ascii:]].*"), 
           Title = str_trim(Title)) |>
    filter_out(Title == "") |>
    select(year, 
           period, 
           period_start, 
           period_end, 
           Title, 
           -half, 
           Runtime, 
           Views, 
           `Hours Viewed`, 
           `Release Date`,
           `Available Globally?`, 
           live_event) |>
    rename(
      title = Title,
      runtime = Runtime,
      views = Views,
      hours_viewed = 'Hours Viewed',
      release_date = 'Release Date',
      available_globally = 'Available Globally?',
    ) |>
  mutate(
    year = as.integer(year),
    available_globally = available_globally == "Yes"
  )
}

Corporate Strategy Memo on Netflix Original Content

Based on the preceding analysis, I recommend a continued prioritization of series-based original content to global audiences, with a specific focus on established series demonstrating an ability to maintain viewer engagement across seasons. The clearest indicator of the popularity of this type of content is reflected in the fact that series-based content accounted for 88.5 percent to 89.1 percent of total viewing hours between the second half of 2023 and the first half of 2026. The analysis also suggests that casting a wider net over global audiences can meaningfully expand the viewing reach of this type of content. Considering the role of movies and live events on the platform, although these content types account for a substantially lower share of viewing hours in general, I would not suggest eliminating these types of content from the Netflix lineup. Instead, they should be thoughtfully curated as complementary offerings to provide the variety that allows Netflix to appeal to a broader range of viewers and bring them onto its platform.

Digging deeper into the prioritization of series-based content, several other indicators presented in the preceding analysis provide insight into the types of series that have proven to be most popular among Netflix viewers. It’s not enough to merely host series-based content that fizzles out after the first season or two. To make this point clearer, the table below outlines the titles with the highest minimum hours viewed from the second half of 2023 and the first half of 2026.

Whether viewers returned to the fourth season of Stranger Things for a recap ahead of its final season, or took a walk down memory lane with some old seasons of Breaking Bad or Suits, one thing remains clear: quality series-based content carries a legacy that draws viewers back time and again.

Beyond prioritizing quality series-based content, Netflix should also continue expanding its content to global audiences. The evidence is compelling that content available across geographic markets accounts for a disproportionately large share of total viewing hours. Between 2023 and 2026, those 35.1 percent of Netflix catalog records accounted for just under 52 percent of total hours viewed.

This suggests that expanding the global availability of Netflix content may provide a meaningful opportunity for the company to increase overall engagement and the total number of hours spent on its platform.

As it relates to complementary offerings, it should be noted that although live events and movies comprise a meaningfully smaller share of the Netflix content viewership, I would not altogether recommend eliminating or even reducing their presence on the platform. Together, these complementary offerings account for roughly 7 to 9 percent of total viewing hours in the more complete reporting periods provided in the data.

Although these figures are dwarfed by the comparable figures for series-based content, live events and movies can serve a broader strategic purpose beyond viewing-hour performance or other measurable metrics. These types of content may help Netflix appeal to viewers seeking event-driven or lower-commitment viewing experiences in a way that may broaden Netflix’s audience over time.

To summarize, I suggest that Netflix continue its acquisition of quality series-based content made available to a global audience while curating a thoughtful canon of complements available to its users.
